I have a lot of side projects. Most advice says I should kill them.

Pick a winner. Archive anything that is not growing. Stop spending time on products that will never become companies.

I understand the advice. I do not think every piece of software has to justify itself like a startup.

## Useful is enough

BudJet helps me understand where my money goes. Viddl downloads a video without turning the page into an ad maze. SlovoCard helps me retain the language around me. Earth Roulette turns "where should we go?" into an actual place.

Those products remain useful if the market is small, growth is flat, or I am one of the main users. I would like them to make money. That is not the only reason to keep software working.

## A few users are still users

Analytics makes it easy to decide that a product is too small to matter. The person using it experiences something simpler: it works or it does not.

If somebody reports a broken import, a wrong vocabulary entry, or an upstream change that stopped a download, I usually fix it. I made the product, it is still public, and the report is usually right.

## Maintenance keeps the useful parts alive

A mature small product contains decisions that are expensive to recover after neglect. BudJet has its category model. Earth Roulette has years of destination data. SlovoCard has language variants and corrections. Viddl has compatibility fixes for sites that keep changing.

Delete the product and that knowledge goes with it.

Sometimes deletion is still right. Security can make old software irresponsible. Infrastructure can cost too much. A dependency can become impossible to support. Maintenance is not free.

## Not every project is a startup

A body of work shows recurring interests, better judgment, techniques learned, and mistakes repeated less often. Revenue matters, but it is not the only useful evidence.

Earth Roulette matters partly because it pulled me back into serious hands-on engineering. My first commercial website matters even though Google Panda broke the business. Viddl matters because the deliberately boring interface is the product decision. BudJet matters because daily use forces honesty that a launch demo never will.

Calling all of that a graveyard would be wrong.

## Projects need different kinds of care

Some projects are evolving. Some are maintained. Some need compatibility fixes, dependency updates, support, and the occasional repair. One is old history.

The useful distinction is how I work on each one now. A maintained tool does not need a quarterly roadmap. A primary focus does. An old project needs an honest record, not a fake relaunch.

This is why I prefer "last tended" to "last commit." Work can mean fixing data, answering a user, renewing an integration, checking a deployment, or deciding that no change is needed.

## What I promise

I do not promise that every product will grow or that every rough edge will disappear quickly. I do promise not to call abandoned software maintained.

If I say a product is maintained, I still look after it. If it is imperfect, I should say where. If somebody finds a problem, there should be a way to tell me.

That is what I mean by a living portfolio: businesses, small utilities, old lessons, and software I still stand behind.
